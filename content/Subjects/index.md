## mathematics

The load-bearing subject — everything else on this page borrows its tools.

- analysis & probability — the working toolkit
- linear algebra — [[Notes/vectors-and-eigenthings]], the language of both ML and quantum
- information theory — [[Notes/entropy]], [[Papers/information-theory]]
- later: measure theory, category theory, if I'm feeling brave

## computation

- machine learning — starting from the papers: [[Papers/attention-is-all-you-need]]
- complexity & computability — what can and can't be computed, and why P vs NP is the wrong question to be popular
- type theory & PL — how languages shape what we can say

## quantum computing & cryptography

The intersection I find most alive: [[Notes/quantum-key-distribution]].
Shor's algorithm breaking RSA, the no-cloning theorem making eavesdropping
detectable, QKD turning physics into a security proof. Nothing else mixes
information theory, linear algebra, and paranoia this elegantly.

## gamedev

Games as the total art form — math, systems, psychology, and music in one
artifact. Interested in: procedural generation (noise functions are tiny
cosmologies), physics engines (verlet integration, constraints), and why
some loops feel good. Outer Wilds and Baba Is You on the
[library](../Library) shelf are the current north stars.

## philosophy

Started: [[Thoughts/what-a-note-owes-you]]. Interested in epistemology
(what makes a belief earn its keep), stoicism vs. its critics, philosophy
of technology — what we do to ourselves when we build tools.

## music

Listening as a subject, not just a pastime: [[Thoughts/on-ambient]] — why
ambient works, what leitmotif does, and the information theory of melody.

## art

Light as a subject: see [paintings](../Paintings) — Lorrain through
Parrish. Later: composition basics, why glazing works, how Turner got
away with it.
