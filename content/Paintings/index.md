The theme of this site is borrowed from painters of luminous skies. Not
the paintings as objects — the *light* in them: cobalt depth, gilt haze,
the feeling that the air itself is made of color. Everything here is
public domain, mostly via Wikimedia Commons.

## the ancestors — Claude Lorrain

Parrish's skies had grandparents. Lorrain (1600–1682) invented the kind
of light this whole genre chases: the sun low, the haze golden, everyone
silhouetted against something enormous and calm.

![[lorrain-seaport.jpg]]

*A seaport at sunset. The ships don't matter; the light does. Three
centuries later Parrish glazed this exact sky with more saturation.*

![[lorrain-pastoral.jpg]]

*The pastoral version — same sun, shepherds instead of sailors.*

## the romantics — Friedrich and Turner

Where Lorrain painted light as harmony, the Romantics painted it as
*event*.

![[friedrich-wanderer.jpg]]

*Wanderer above the Sea of Fog, Caspar David Friedrich, 1818. The
patron saint of everyone who has ever stood at the edge of a hard
problem. This is what opening a new subject page here should feel like.*

![[friedrich-monk.jpg]]

*Monk by the Sea, Friedrich, 1810. Almost no picture in it — a figure,
a beach, an enormous sky. The closest painting I know to a blank page.*

![[turner-temeraire.jpg]]

*The Fighting Temeraire, J. M. W. Turner, 1839. A ghost ship towed to
her last berth by a small steam tug, into a sunset that outlives her.
Turner called it his darling; the light is doing all the grieving.*

## my own

This is where my own art goes. The workflow is deliberately simple:

1. Put the file in `content/attachments/` (any name, no spaces).
2. Add a line here: `![[your-file.png]]` followed by an italic caption.
3. Tag yourself `#art` if you want it findable.

Anything counts — a photo with the right sky, a glazed study, a failed
painting that taught you something. The caption is the honest part: one
line on what you were chasing.
