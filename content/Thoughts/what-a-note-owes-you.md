title: what a note owes you
description: A belief written down has obligations to its future reader — you.

A note is a claim made to a stranger. The stranger is you in eight months,
with no memory of the context that made the claim feel obvious. Most
note-taking systems fail not from bad tools but from notes written for the
person you are now instead of the person you'll be.

So every note owes four things:

1. **A title that is a claim.** "Entropy" is a drawer. "Entropy measures
   uncertainty, and uncertainty is the thing being compressed" is a belief
   you can agree or disagree with.
2. **One idea.** If a note contains two ideas, it's two notes. (The
   zettelkasten reason: links are cheap, separations are not.)
3. **The why.** Where did this come from — a book, a conversation, a
   mistake? Future-you needs to weigh the evidence.
4. **An address.** A link to at least one other note. An unlinked note is
   a sentence said to an empty room.

This is [[Thoughts/zettelkasten-how|zettelkasten]] thinking applied to
honesty rather than productivity: the system's real product is that it
makes vagueness *visible*.
